01About

From Schwarzschild horizons to persistence diagrams — the same geometric language, different domains.

My academic training is in mathematical physics — an MSc from the University of Edinburgh (2024), preceded by a BSc in Physics at the University of Debrecen on a Stipendium Hungaricum scholarship. My focus is the geometry of gravitating systems — Killing horizons, conformal structures, and the deep role of gauge symmetry in defining what physical degrees of freedom even are. My MSc dissertation was on the Penrose & Hawking-Penrose singularity theorems — how global topology forces black-hole and big-bang singularities. I’m especially drawn to the boundary where general relativity meets algebraic topology: characteristic classes, fibre bundles, and how spacetime topology constrains the local physics allowed on it.

Outside the seminar room, I build models. The same fibre-bundle thinking that organises a Yang-Mills theory turns out to be exactly the right lens for Geometric Deep Learning: equivariant networks, message passing on graphs, and gauge-equivariant CNNs on manifolds. And the algebraic-topology toolkit — homology, persistent homology, the Mapper algorithm — shows up again as Topological Data Analysis, where it makes sense of the shape of high-dimensional point clouds and neural-network latent spaces.

I’m currently open to contract and research-collaboration work as an ML architect on GDL- and TDA-flavoured problems — from prototyping to production architecture.
